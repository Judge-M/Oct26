# Incoming SOC shift handover

EXERCISE ONLY. At exercise time 09:30Z, the outgoing shift reports suspected
disclosure of movement details for fictional patrol LANTERN, already in the field.
The patrol's current movements, the exposed version, access method, and remaining
affected systems have not been established. No confirmed harm has been reported.

The outgoing shift collected endpoint, network, identity, document-service and SIEM
exports. An account password was reset at 09:20Z. Do not assume the incident ended.

## How your team works

- Open the shared **IRIS** queue and claim an available ticket. Your team owns one
  ticket at a time; every ticket has exactly one owner.
- Open **CTFd** to see the questions for your owned ticket, then investigate the
  released evidence on your team desktop (Autopsy, Wireshark, Cutter, Wazuh) as
  each question directs. Use the beginner guide and free hints whenever needed.
- Submit answers in CTFd. Each globally accepted correct answer earns one point
  and automatically publishes its evidence-backed finding into the shared IRIS
  case for every team to read. Completing a ticket unlocks follow-up work.
- If another team should continue a ticket, relinquish it in IRIS. Solved
  questions stay solved and points remain with the team that earned them.

There is no report to write, no status to set by hand, and no facilitator
approval step — the findings you publish are your contribution to the joint
assessment, and the after-action review builds on them.

## Rules of engagement

Do not execute evidence files or connect to addresses found in evidence. Do not
scan external systems. All addresses, identities, places and artifacts are
fictional; no attack is required or permitted. Use UTC for evidence and elapsed
minutes for exercise deadlines. Label claims as confirmed, assessed (with
confidence), or unknown — report potential relevance to LANTERN without
inventing harm, adversary identity, or real-world movements.

Success means a defensible shared assessment built on cited evidence, not speed
alone. Keep an explicit unanswered-questions list; uncertainty is a finding too.
